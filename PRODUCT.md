# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Game-server owners, builders, and trusted operators who need to inspect, create, and safely change live game servers, websites, and small services. They use the web control room and agent surfaces to check workload health, create approved templates or custom containers, review proposals, author Minecraft rules, and manage their own account without sharing authority with other operators.

## Product Purpose

indexd is the authenticated web control room for the Farlands Live workload platform. It turns the backend's scoped action space into a clear desktop interface for Minecraft, dedicated games, Node sites and services, and bounded custom OCI containers. Success means every screen reflects live per-user data, every control reaches the real API under the signed-in user's identity, and no private workload or player information is exposed before authentication.

## Brand Personality

Grounded, playful, trustworthy. The cherry-panorama desktop and Minecraft-inspired controls should feel familiar to server owners, while copy and state handling remain calm and operationally precise.

## Anti-references

Do not turn the product into a generic SaaS dashboard, a neon gaming control panel, or a fake desktop filled with decorative controls. Never present fixture data as live data, hide destructive consequences, use vague AI claims, or expose raw cluster and infrastructure details to users.

## Design Principles

1. Playful surface, serious boundaries. The world can feel game-like while authentication, ownership, approval, and error states remain explicit.
2. Live truth over theatre. Counts, health, realms, and user identity come from real connectors or clearly say they are unavailable.
3. One operator, one scope. Every request carries the current account's session and the backend remains the final authorization boundary.
4. Safe actions explain themselves. Controls show pending, success, refusal, and recovery states without implying that an unimplemented action succeeded.
5. Familiar interaction wins. Windows, buttons, account controls, and forms remain keyboard-accessible and predictable.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support keyboard navigation, visible focus, reduced motion, status communication that does not rely on color alone, readable contrast, descriptive labels, and a responsive login and desktop experience.
